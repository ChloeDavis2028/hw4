# Design: Campus Customs Air (Problem 10)

**Concept:** a real Campus Customs storefront with an aviation twist. Shoppers are pilots, pages are flights, and Chloe is the control tower. It's built to welcome everyone: Yale students, families, alumni, New Haven neighbors, and young aviators of every background.

| Area | What changed | Why it keeps customers around to buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Fonts** | Playfair Display for headlines (collegiate, Yale feel); Inter for body text; Saira Stencil for labels, like aircraft stenciling; JetBrains Mono for radio and instrument readouts. Loaded from Google Fonts. | Clear, readable text with a distinct personality. Each font has one job, so shoppers instantly know headline, info and "instrument" apart. |
| **Colors** | Defined tokens: Yale Blue `#00356B` (brand), cockpit navy `#0F1B2D` (header, chat, board), flight-deck amber `#F2B632` (actions, highlights), runway red `#C8102E` (stamps, last call), signal green `#3DDC84` (boarding, online). | Amber always means "act here" and red always means "hurry", so buttons and urgency are easy to spot. Consistent Yale Blue builds trust in an official store. |
| **Capitalization (academic style)** | One Chicago-style rule site-wide: headings, buttons and menu items in Title Case without closing periods ("Wear Your Bulldog Spirit Every Day", "Log Out", "Clear Filters"); form labels and body text in sentence case; proper names always capitalized (Handsome Dan, Hall of Flight, Yale Tower); "vs." lowercase. "Elis" became "Yale students" so every visitor understands it. | Consistent, scholarly typography reads as a credible, official Yale-area store. Plain words welcome visitors who don't know Yale slang. |
| **Hierarchy** | Home flows: hero → **live departures board** → categories → **Hall of Flight** → Chloe. Item page: big photo left; name, price and details right; **boarding pass** for fare and sizes. | The first screen shows real products and prices, not just slogans. The two things a buyer checks most, price and their size, sit together in one bold block. |
| **Motion** | Split-flap letters on the departures board; C-130 flight between pages; pages rise in; cards taxi in; price radar ping; flashing "LAST CALL"; size boxes light up. All switch off for visitors who choose reduced motion. | Motion guides the eye to products and urgency, and makes browsing feel like an experience. It's accessible for motion-sensitive visitors. |
| **Product presentation** | Departures board lists real items as "flights": fare = price, status from real stock ("LAST CALL · 9 left"). Item pages show a **boarding pass** (FROM HVN → TO YOU, FARE, size boxes, flight stub, barcode). Cards keep the fun names, cargo-drop reels, and "Dan approved" badge. | Low-stock "last call" creates honest urgency (115 size slots have only 1–5 left). Presenting the purchase as a trip ("to your closet") makes buying feel exciting, not transactional. |
| **Chat feel** | Chloe's panel is a cockpit radio: speaker grill, "Chloe · Yale Tower", callsigns, **Zulu-time stamps** ("1124Z"), "Push to talk" prompt, **📻 Transmit** button. | A memorable, on-theme helper invites questions. Every answer uses live prices and stock, and each question that gets answered is one less reason to leave. |
| **Aviation culture for all** | "YALE" in the pilot alphabet (Yankee · Alpha · Lima · Echo). "All aboard: no Yale ID required." **Hall of Flight**: Harriet Quimby (1911), Bessie Coleman (1921), Amelia Earhart (1932), Sally Ride (1983), Eileen Collins (1999). **"Future flyers welcome: earn your wings"** links to sign-up (rank + callsign). | Speaks to new audiences: young aviators, women and girls who love flight, families and visitors, not just current students. Inspiration plus a clear next step ("Earn your wings") turns visitors into account holders who come back. |
| **Real cargo drops (About page)** | "The Real Cargo Drop" gallery: 3 public-domain U.S. Air Force photos of C-130s airdropping humanitarian bundles in Operation Christmas Drop (Fais 2015, Fassari 2024, Oneop 2023), each with photographer credit and a Wikimedia Commons link. | Real aircraft and a real humanitarian mission make the theme authentic, and explain the cargo-drop reels on every card. |
| **Inspiration in chat** | Chloe encourages anyone who wants to fly. She gives one real first step (a discovery flight, Civil Air Patrol cadets, EAA Young Eagles, Women in Aviation International, math and science), points to the Hall of Flight, and suggests "earn your wings". | Turns a dream into a next step, and a visitor into a returning, signed-up customer who feels welcome. |

**Built on earlier work:** cockpit nav, C-130 flights, Chloe's ATC voice, radar product cards, cargo-drop reels, the phone flight menu and filters (Problem 9).
